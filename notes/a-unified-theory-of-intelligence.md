# A unified theory of intelligence

Working notes toward a theorem. Eric Kryski, with drafting and literature checks by Claude, 2026-10-17. Not a paper: the manuscript that may come out of these notes is a later step, and nothing here has been run.

## 0. Status and scope

These notes take the oscillator half of the 2026-10-17 conversation with Google AI Mode (`source-transcript-2026-10-17.md`, lines 1046 to 2265) and sort it into what is a claim, what the evidence for each claim is, what is already published, what was wrong, and what to run. The conversation is kept as the record of how the ideas were unlocked (Section 3), because the questions in it are the questions, and the search engine's replies are treated as prompts to check rather than as evidence: they were generous to a fault, and several of the things they confirmed are false (Sections 3, 4 and 9).

Sources used: the transcript; paper 01, the survey ([Kryski 2026a](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7445198)), whose Sections 1.2.2, 3.1.3 to 3.1.5 and Appendix B already cover much of the ground and are not repeated; paper 02, the untrained-reservoir study (`papers/02-untrained-reservoirs/spoken-digit-recognition-without-training.md`), whose record gives the precise prior numbers; three reviews written for these notes on 2026-10-17 (a theory triage of which claims can be propositions, an experiment design for the connectome track, and a survey of nervous-system datasets beyond the fly); and reference checks against publisher and arXiv records on the same date. The few facts still marked "to confirm" are listed at the end of Section 17.

What "a unified theory of intelligence" claims here, in one paragraph. Intelligence, biological and synthetic, is the behaviour of a network of coupled nonlinear oscillators that operates near its synchronization transition; its units carry information in phase and amplitude and in the relations between them, not in a scalar; its thresholds are bifurcations the units pass through rather than functions applied to them; it is organized as modules of dense local coupling joined by sparse long-range coupling; it is kept out of global lock and out of chaos by dissipation, inhibition and slow rhythms that periodically reset it; it learns by local rules that follow from an energy function; and its failures of rhythm are its diseases. The deepest form of the claim, that the electrochemical account of the neuron is one level of description and not the mechanism, is the premise Eric holds and the least supported part; it is stated in Section 2 and examined in Appendix B, and the rest of the notes are written so that nothing in Tiers 1 and 2 waits on it.

What the notes do not claim: any experimental result beyond papers 01 and 02; any proof beyond the propositions of Section 5, of which only one is new; any result on a connectome.

## 1. Verdict

The question asked was whether this is really something, and the honest answer has four parts.

**What is coherent and worth a serious program.** A Stuart–Landau network with a per-unit bifurcation parameter μ_i, symmetric coupling and a common frequency is a gradient system (Proposition P3). In it, thresholds and couplings can be trained by local rules, the operating point sits on a known phase diagram and can be measured by the order parameter and by information processing capacity, and every claimed effect has a named control. The modular architecture (dense local lattices, sparse long-range coupling, slow modulation) is the regime paper 01 identified as the useful one and the regime cortex appears to use. Instantiating a real connectome's wiring as the coupling of such a network, driving it through its sensory neurons and reading its motor neurons against rewired twins, has not been done with oscillator units, and the fly, the zebrafish hindbrain, C. elegans and the stomatogastric ganglion each offer a quantitative target for it. That is small, falsifiable, and it extends paper 02's harness rather than replacing it. It is enough to be a research program, and a good one.

**What is already in the literature and must be built on, not rediscovered.** Units on spheres with synchrony as binding (AKOrN, [Miyato et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13821); the D-dimensional Kuramoto model of [Chandra, Girvan & Ott 2019](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.9.011002)); the D-dimensional amplitude oscillator Eric described, already defined and solved in physics ([Zou et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.107202); [Gogoi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.110.L032202), [Gogoi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/kg1l-8dvc)); the threshold as a Hopf bifurcation, which is what the Stuart–Landau model is, and the compressive gain at the critical point, which is the Hopf-cochlea literature ([Camalet et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.97.7.3183); [Eguíluz et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.84.5232)); sparsity in trained oscillator fields (Un-0's posts, not peer-reviewed); dissipation as the condition for trainability (coRNN, [Rusch & Mishra 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00951); paper 01 §3.1.4); equilibrium propagation on oscillator networks ([Rageau & Grollier 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4386/adebaa); [Wang, Wanjura & Marquardt 2024](https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4386/ad752b)); the capacity bound for any linear readout of a dynamical system ([Dambre et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00514)); self-tuned criticality in sensory oscillators (Camalet); connectome-constrained models of the fly ([Lappalainen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3); [Shiu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9); [Jin et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997)); Kuramoto dynamics on the fly connectome as a criticality study with no task ([Ódor, Papp & Deco 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chaos.2026.119150)); the second-order oscillator recurrences that the transcript's "driven damped wave equation" names without knowing it (coRNN; Neural Wave Machines, [Keller & Welling 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/keller23a.html)); region-level Hopf and Kuramoto models of whole mammalian brains, which are a mature field ([Cabral et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.04.010); [Deco et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-03073-5)). None of this diminishes the program. It fixes where the program's novelty can sit: in the unit's bifurcation parameter trained locally, in the operating point measured rather than assumed, in a functional oscillator model on real wiring at synaptic scale, and in the pre-specified pathology mapping.

**What was inflated or wrong in the conversation.** "A massive validation of your core thesis" for the paper 02 result (Section 4 has the numbers: the untrained Stuart–Landau network reaches the range of small trained networks in noise at one gain and does not beat them, and a leaky-integrator bank matches its memory). The torus as "a geometric truth of nature" (the effect is under a point, and three different tori were run together; Section 9). "Localized Lagrangians" and "least action" for driven, dissipative systems (Section 15). The skin-effect account of theta and myelin (Appendix B). "The ball of yarn is topologically a torus" and "audio was born on a torus" (meaningless). Capacity "exponentially higher" per oscillator (false at matched state dimension; P1). "Almost zero architectural tuning" for a connectome-wired network (Shiu calibrated a synaptic weight against a firing-rate target; Lappalainen fitted 734 parameters). And "unified theory of how the brain works" as a conclusion rather than a conjecture. The conjecture is kept, as Eric's; the conclusion is not yet earned.

**The strongest objections a reviewer will raise, and what answers each.** (1) Capacity is bookkeeping: an oscillator holds two or D real numbers and the total linear capacity is bounded by that count, so "more features per unit" is a unit-count illusion. Answer: compare at matched state dimension and FLOPs, never at unit count, and run the decodability test (E0d) in which the claim becomes about allocation and readability rather than total. (2) Equilibrium propagation needs the network to settle, and the operating point the theory wants is partial coherence, short of settling. Answer: measure the trade-off directly (E0a with E0c) and, where it bites, use the holomorphic variant or a non-energy rule, and say so. (3) The Stuart–Landau gain in paper 02 is the amplitude absorbing the drive, and a damped linear resonator does that too. Answer: a damped linear resonator bank is a control on every Stuart–Landau result (E0e). (4) Within-cell-type rewired twins may match the connectome everywhere, in which case the result is "cell-type block structure is a good prior", which Un-0's sparsity post and the fly graph models already say. Answer: pre-specify that twin as the one to beat, and accept the reduced claim if it ties.

**Bottom line.** Eric is on to a program, not yet a theory. The pieces are real, most of them are published, and their combination into one substrate for architecture, training, biology and pathology is the bold move, which has coherence on its side and no evidence yet beyond papers 01 and 02. The title of these notes is earned by E0 through E5 and their falsifiers (Section 12), not by argument.

## 2. The theorem, stated

### 2.1 The conjecture, in Eric's words

From the conversation and from the review of these notes: "This has now started to frame how I think we should structure an oscillator model, by chaining oscillators, how we should train them, why they're similar to biological neurons, how to build them like biological brains, and how biological brains work and how neurological conditions work, and how we might run experiments to show we can do the same thing with a biologically inspired oscillator brain. All of this is rooted on the premise that we've been thinking about the brain all wrong with Hodgkin–Huxley electrochemical model as the primary mechanism for how brains work. It's actually a physics problem. Fluid and quantum mechanics, and neurons are complex oscillators. So synthetic brains should be the same."

### 2.2 The claim ladder

Each claim carries a status: **[known]** means it is established and cited, and the notes build on it; **[hypothesis]** means it is testable and names its decisive test; **[speculative]** means it is quarantined in Appendix B with its counter-evidence. The theorem is the conjunction of the Tier 1 and Tier 2 claims; Tier 3 is the premise Eric holds and is not needed by the others.

**Tier 1: computational.**

- **T1.1 Capacity and readability.** At matched state dimension, units with phase and amplitude (or a D-dimensional state) organize representational capacity so that more task-relevant features are linearly readable from the units' own coordinates than from scalar units, and reading phase adds to reading amplitude. **[hypothesis]** The total capacity is bounded by the state count (P1, known); the claim is about allocation. Decisive test: E0d (toy superposition at matched state dimension; phase plus amplitude against amplitude alone against scalar plus sparse autoencoder) and E2 (odour discrimination in the mushroom body wiring). Falsified if reading amplitude alone equals reading amplitude and phase, or if the scalar layer at matched states ties.
- **T1.2 Threshold as bifurcation.** A unit whose activation is a bifurcation with a per-unit parameter μ_i, trained by a local rule, matches or beats a fixed nonlinearity at matched parameters. **[hypothesis]**, resting on a definition (P2, known: μ_i crossing zero is the Stuart–Landau threshold) and one new proposition (P3: the local rule). Decisive test: E0c. Falsified if a fixed tanh or ReLU gate at matched states ties learned μ_i.
- **T1.3 Operating point.** Task performance peaks at partial coherence, with the order parameter r strictly between 0 and 1, falling on both sides. **[hypothesis]**, on a known phase diagram (P4). Decisive test: E0a. Falsified if accuracy is flat in r or best at full lock.
- **T1.4 Modular chaining.** Modules of dense local coupling joined by sparse long-range coupling outperform one dense module at matched states, and the inter-module coupling learns to bind one module's output to the next module's input. **[hypothesis]** Prior art: Un-0's modular sparsity (not peer-reviewed); the exponential distance rule of cortical wiring ([Ercsey-Ravasz et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.036)). Decisive test: the chaining arm of E0b, then E2 to E4. Falsified if one dense module at the same state count, or the rewired inter-module twin, reads as well.
- **T1.5 Local training.** Energy-based and local rules (equilibrium propagation, contrastive Hebbian learning, Hebbian phase plasticity, frequency learning) reach the accuracy of backpropagation through time at matched trained parameters, with better stability. **[hypothesis]** Prior art: Rageau & Grollier 2025. Decisive test: E0c. Falsified if backpropagation with Adam matches every local method at matched parameters and wall-clock; a remaining advantage in hardware plausibility would be a different claim.

**Tier 2: biological correspondence.**

- **T2.1** A neuron near its firing threshold is a limit-cycle oscillator, and populations of them reduce to phase and amplitude models. **[known]** ([Stiefel & Ermentrout 2016](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00525.2015); [Breakspear, Heitmann & Daffertshofer 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2010.00190); exact mean field for spiking populations, [Montbrió, Pazó & Roxin 2015](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.021028)). Paper 01 §1.2.2 sets this out.
- **T2.2** Communication and binding between groups are implemented by synchrony, gated by slower rhythms (communication through coherence; theta–gamma coupling). **[known as a neuroscience hypothesis with evidence]** ([Fries 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034); [Lisman & Jensen 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.007)). The testable machine-learning form, already posed in paper 01 §3.2, is whether the coupling and decoupling of a trained modular oscillator network falls into comparable gating relations. **[hypothesis]** Test: measure cross-frequency coupling in the chained network of E0b and E2.
- **T2.3** Disorders of rhythm are failures of dynamical regime (thalamocortical dysrhythmia, Parkinsonian beta locking, seizure as global synchrony, tinnitus-like self-sustained loops), and removing the same controls from the model produces the same signatures. **[hypothesis]** Prior art: [Llinás et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.96.26.15222); [Uhlhaas & Singer 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.09.020); coordinated reset, [Tass 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00422-003-0425-7); region-level Hopf models of Parkinson's stimulation ([Saenger et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-10003-y)) and of epilepsy (the Virtual Epileptic Patient, [Jirsa et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.04.049)). What would be new: a synaptic-scale model with a pre-specified map from perturbation to signature (Section 10). Falsified if the predicted signature does not appear, or appears equally under a sham perturbation of the same size.
- **T2.4** Real connectome wiring, instantiated as the coupling of an oscillator network, does functional work beyond what rewired twins do. **[hypothesis]** Prior art with other units: leaky integrate-and-fire (Shiu 2024), fitted rate units (Lappalainen 2024), graph message passing (Jin 2026); Kuramoto on the fly connectome without a task (Ódor, Papp & Deco 2026). Decisive tests: E1 (heading ring), E2, E4, E5. Falsified if the within-cell-type rewired twin ties.

**Tier 3: the biophysical premise.** Quarantined in Appendix B.

- **T3.1** The action potential is primarily a mechanical and thermodynamic wave in the membrane, and the electrical signal is secondary. **[speculative]** A live minority position ([Heimburg & Jackson 2005](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0503823102)) with evidence on both sides; paper 01 §1.2.2 records it and takes no position.
- **T3.2** Myelin acts as a frequency filter; theta penetrates it or cancels gamma harmonically. **[speculative, and as physics unsupported]** The demyelination result it was attached to is real and has a different mechanism ([Dubey et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.73827)).
- **T3.3** Quantum mechanics plays a computational role in neurons. **[speculative]** No evidence; the strongest version is [Fisher 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aop.2015.08.020); the decoherence objection is [Tegmark 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194).

### 2.3 What would prove or disprove each tier

Tier 1 is proved, in the only sense available to it, by E0: five screening experiments on paper 02's harness, each with a falsifier (Section 12). Any one of T1.1 to T1.5 can fail without the others, and the theorem survives with that claim struck; the theory does not survive if T1.1 and T1.3 both fail, because then an oscillator is a leaky integrator with a radius, which is paper 02's own reading of its result. Tier 2 is proved by E1 through E5: a wired oscillator model that beats the within-type rewired twin on a quantitative target, in at least two nervous systems, and reproduces a pre-specified pathology signature under the matching perturbation. It is disproved by ties against rewired twins everywhere, which would reduce the connectome's contribution to its cell-type statistics. Tier 3 is neither proved nor disproved by anything in these notes; Appendix B names the experiments in the biophysics literature that bear on it. The dependency is one-way: Tiers 1 and 2 are formulated and tested in classical dynamical systems and do not need Tier 3. If Tier 3 were ever established it would make the oscillator description of a neuron the literal one rather than a reduction, which is how paper 01 already phrases the stakes.

**Turn 14 (line 1048). Asked:** Oscillatory networks trained with Adam work, but is loss-function training appropriate for them? Could an oscillator encode more features if its position relative to its peers and its coupling were read, and if the physics went beyond phase to amplitude and a third axis, in a polar system centred in a sphere? Would the activation threshold then be a Lagrangian limit that emerges in training? **The reply said:** a three-dimensional spherical oscillator gives phase for relations, amplitude for salience and a polar angle for abstraction; the activation becomes a Hopf bifurcation boundary; backpropagation through time is unstable and physically implausible; use equilibrium propagation, spike-timing plasticity or contrastive Hebbian learning; beware global synchronization. **What holds:** the Hopf bifurcation as the threshold is exactly what a Stuart–Landau unit does when its parameter μ crosses zero (P2); equilibrium propagation, contrastive Hebbian learning and Hebbian phase plasticity exist for oscillator networks (Section 7); the synchronization trap is the catastrophic lock paper 01 §3.1.4 names. **Inflated or wrong:** "a single physical oscillator can represent an entire trajectory of superposed features" (the capacity of any linear readout is bounded by the number of state variables, P1); the polar angle "encodes hierarchical abstraction" (no basis); "physically implausible" is not an argument against backpropagation as a computation, and the trained oscillator literature uses it (coRNN; [Chandravadia & Imam 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2026.101563)).

**Turn 15 (line 1119). Asked:** Is there physics beyond three dimensions and would it help? What does a readout look like, and how is signal told from noise? How is the readout made performant for a model that runs in real time? How would you train it? **The reply said:** hyperspheres, quaternions and complex spaces; read the order parameter and a phase-difference matrix into a shallow classifier; sample events (threshold or zero crossings) into a spiking or echo-state readout; train with an unsupervised Hebbian tier followed by equilibrium propagation. **What holds:** units on S^(D−1) are the D-dimensional Kuramoto model ([Chandra, Girvan & Ott 2019](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.9.011002)) and AKOrN uses them; the order parameter and the phase-locking value are standard and paper 03 measures both; event-driven readouts are neuromorphic practice. **Inflated:** "exponentially many nearly orthogonal directions" is a property of any high-dimensional space, not of oscillators; the two-tier protocol is a reasonable plan presented as settled.

**Turn 16 (lines 1195 and 1199, with a slide on manifolds and the Lagrangian). Asked:** People say models are, or should be, manifolds or knotted tori; is there anything to that? The thesis: the world is wave-based, neurons evolved to it, neurons that fire together synchronize in gamma and are reset by theta, so an artificial network should be modelled on the same mechanisms and would understand the world better, be more efficient and pack more concepts into fewer units. Be very critical. Would the geometric feature space make units effectively monosemantic when read by phase and amplitude? Could oscillator reservoirs be chained, sensory to conceptual to output? For equilibrium propagation, is the Lagrangian computed or does the network settle? **The reply said:** the manifold hypothesis is standard; N phases live on an N-torus and a learned concept is a knot on it; the thesis is beautiful but bottlenecked by the Von Neumann architecture and by chaos; prove it with a matched-budget Pareto benchmark; the unit is polysemantic but its instantaneous state can be monosemantic; chaining mirrors retina to cortex; equilibrium propagation lets the network settle. **What holds:** the manifold hypothesis; the N-torus state space (Section 9); the two critiques (paper 01 §3.1.4); the matched benchmark as the right test (and paper 02 is one); equilibrium propagation's free phase is a settling. **Inflated or wrong:** "capacity exponentially higher than a static network of the same size" (false at matched state dimension, P1); "perfectly monosemantic" by phase sampling is untested (E0d is the test); the knot picture is a mode-locked periodic orbit, not a learned concept (Section 15).

**Turn 20 (line 1642). Asked:** On the collision test: locked alpha and gamma block other signals until a theta reset, so only some frequencies lock and only some unlock. **Paper 02, single-digit recognition** (`spoken-digit-recognition-without-training.md` §4.1). ## 5. Objects and propositions

### 5.1 Objects

**Stuart–Landau network.** N units with complex state z_i, ż_i = (μ_i + iω_i) z_i − |z_i|² z_i + Σ_j K_ij z_j + u_i(t). Paper 02's Stuart–Landau coupling is diffusive, Σ_j K_ij (z_j − z_i), which is the same equation with the self term folded into μ_i. Phase-only Kuramoto is the limit in which |z_i| is held at 1.

**D-dimensional Stuart–Landau unit.** State x_i ∈ R^D, ẋ_i = (μ_i − |x_i|²) x_i + Ω_i x_i + Σ_j K_ij x_j + u_i, with Ω_i antisymmetric (a rotation generator). For D = 2 and Ω_i = ω_i J it is the Stuart–Landau unit above. For D = 3, Ω_i has a null axis: on the invariant sphere |x_i|² = μ_i the state rotates about that axis at the unit's frequency, so the unit carries a phase (longitude), an amplitude (radius) and a latitude that is neutrally stable and is set by coupling and input. This is the object Eric's "phase, amplitude and z" names. Its unit-norm projection, ẋ_i = Ω_i x_i + P_{x_i}(c_i + Σ_j K_ij x_j) with P the projection onto the tangent space of the sphere, is the D-dimensional Kuramoto model ([Chandra, Girvan & Ott 2019](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.9.011002)) and the update AKOrN uses. The amplitude-carrying D-dimensional form is defined and solved in the physics literature: globally coupled high-dimensional limit-cycle oscillators ẋ = (1 − |x|²)x + Wx with W antisymmetric, which reduce to the D-dimensional Kuramoto model at weak coupling ([Zou et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.130.107202)); exactly solvable Stuart–Landau models in arbitrary dimension ([Gogoi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.110.L032202)); and coupled D-dimensional Stuart–Landau oscillators in D = 3 and 4, with synchronization, multistability and partial quenching ([Gogoi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/kg1l-8dvc)). No machine-learning use of the model was found, which is where the program's contribution would sit.

**Readout.** A linear map on features of the state. Paper 02 reads windowed mean, standard deviation and mean absolute change of cos θ_i and sin θ_i (and of x_i, y_i for Stuart–Landau), projects to a common width by a fixed random matrix and fits a closed-form ridge. AKOrN reads norms of a learned projection of the final vectors.

**The matched-M rule.** Two networks are compared at the same number of state variables M (M = 2N for Stuart–Landau or a phase network read as cosine and sine, M = ND for D-dimensional units, M = the bank's states for a leaky-integrator bank), the same FLOPs per step, the same readout width and the same count of trained parameters. Unit count is never the unit of comparison.

### 5.2 Propositions

**P1, capacity bound (the reason for the matched-M rule).** For a dynamical system with M state variables read by a linear map, with fading memory, the total information processing capacity summed over all functions of the input history is at most M, with equality when the state variables are linearly independent ([Dambre et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00514)). Corollary: a Stuart–Landau network (M = 2N), a phase network read as cosine and sine (M = 2N), a D-dimensional network (M = ND) and a leaky-integrator bank with M states share one ceiling. What an architecture can claim is the allocation of that capacity between memory and nonlinear functions, never the total. Not new; it is the theorem behind paper 02's state-matched bank and AKOrN's rule of 256/N channels per oscillator dimension. Violated by: nonlinear readouts (paper 02's standard deviation and mean absolute change are nonlinear in the state; a phase-difference feature cos(θ_i − θ_j) is quadratic in it); regimes without fading memory; and full lock, where in the rotating frame the state is a fixed point, memory capacity goes to zero and everything is allocated to instantaneous nonlinear functions of the input. T1.1 is a claim about allocation and about readability in the units' own coordinates; it is not a claim about total capacity.

**P2, threshold as bifurcation parameter (a definition, plus one scaling).** For an isolated Stuart–Landau unit, μ < 0 gives a stable origin and a linear resonant response with gain of order 1/|μ|; μ > 0 gives a limit cycle of radius √μ; at μ = 0 a forcing of amplitude F produces a stationary amplitude |z| = F^(1/3), the compressive nonlinearity the hearing literature derives from the same normal form ([Eguíluz et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.84.5232); [Camalet et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.97.7.3183)). The only content beyond the definition is what sets μ_i: fixed by design; learned (P3); self-tuned by feedback so the unit sits at its critical point, Camalet's self-tuned criticality; or state-dependent, μ_i = μ_0 − β Σ_{j ∈ N(i)} |z_j|², which is local inhibition acting as a soft winner-take-all. The last two are hypotheses. Hazard: with heterogeneous ω_i and strong coupling a Stuart–Landau population can enter amplitude death, every unit at z = 0 ([Mirollo & Strogatz 1990](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01013676); [Matthews, Mirollo & Strogatz 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(91)90129-W)), the amplitude analogue of catastrophic lock and a candidate explanation for the Stuart–Landau network losing accuracy in paper 02 at strong restoring strength and input gain (to test).

**P3, energy function and local rules (the new piece, small).** Take a common frequency ω, the rotating frame w_i = z_i e^(−iωt), real symmetric K, and arbitrary μ_i. Define E(w) = Σ_i (−μ_i |w_i|² + ½ |w_i|⁴) − 2 Σ_{i<j} K_ij Re(w̄_i w_j), with an input term −2 Σ_i Re(ū_i w_i) for an input constant in the rotating frame. Then ẇ_i = −∂E/∂w̄_i, dE/dt = −2 Σ_i |ẇ_i|² ≤ 0, and E is bounded below by its quartic term, so by LaSalle's principle every trajectory converges to the set of equilibria. This is the gradient structure of the real Ginzburg–Landau equation ([Aranson & Kramer 2002](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.74.99)) and the amplitude counterpart of the Kuramoto Lyapunov function ([van Hemmen & Wreszinski 1993](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01048044)). Equilibrium propagation ([Scellier & Bengio 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2017.00024)) then applies as written: with a free equilibrium w^0 and an equilibrium w^β under a nudge of strength β toward the target, every parameter updates by the contrast of its energy derivative, and since ∂E/∂μ_i = −|w_i|² and ∂E/∂K_ij = −2 Re(w̄_i w_j), the updates are local: Δμ_i ∝ (|w_i^β|² − |w_i^0|²)/β, an amplitude-contrast rule for the threshold, and ΔK_ij ∝ (Re(w̄_i w_j)^β − Re(w̄_i w_j)^0)/β, a phase-coherence-contrast rule for the coupling. [Rageau & Grollier 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4386/adebaa) (Neuromorphic Computing and Engineering 5(3):034008) train couplings and drive terms in Kuramoto and amplitude-plus-phase networks by equilibrium propagation, state no explicit symmetry requirement for the couplings (the energy-gradient form implies it), and report that the training itself drives the network toward synchrony even from widely dispersed frequencies; whether they train a per-unit bifurcation parameter is to be confirmed by reading the paper's appendix. If not, "thresholds learned by a local rule inside the Hopf normal form" is the one proposition in these notes that is both new and provable. Violated by: heterogeneous ω_i (the term iΔω_i w_i has no real potential, so convergence becomes empirical; Rageau & Grollier report successful training from widely dispersed initial frequencies, with the training itself pulling the network toward synchrony, by simulation and a heuristic argument rather than a proof); asymmetric K (every trained AKOrN and Un-0 coupling, and every connectome), for which [Laborieux & Zenke 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02214) relax the symmetry requirement through Jacobian homeostasis, the route to test on directed wiring; delays. Holomorphic equilibrium propagation ([Laborieux & Zenke 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00530)) applies, since E is polynomial in the real coordinates and continues analytically, but its oscillations are the teaching signal and it keeps the requirement that the network settle. That requirement is the tension with T1.3: the operating point the theory wants is short of settling. Section 12 (E0a with E0c) measures the trade-off rather than assuming it away.

**P4, the synchronization transition and the operating point.** For all-to-all Kuramoto with Lorentzian frequencies of half-width γ, the critical coupling is K_c = 2γ and the order parameter above it is r = (1 − K_c/K)^(1/2) ([Kuramoto 1975](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0013365); exact reduction in [Ott & Antonsen 2008](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2930766)). For Stuart–Landau units with heterogeneous frequencies the diagram has incoherence, locking, amplitude death and a Hopf regime (Matthews, Mirollo & Strogatz 1991). For D-dimensional units the transition is continuous for even D and discontinuous, at zero coupling, for odd D (Chandra, Girvan & Ott 2019), so "just before lock" is a different kind of point for D = 3 than for D = 2 or 4. "Capacity peaks at partial coherence" is therefore a hypothesis about where on a known diagram a known quantity peaks, in the tradition of computation at the order–chaos boundary ([Bertschinger & Natschläger 2004](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976604323057443); [Legenstein & Maass 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2007.04.017)), and not a theorem.

**P5, dissipativity.** With ‖K‖ ≤ κ in operator norm and |u_i| ≤ U, d/dt ‖z‖² ≤ 2(μ_max + κ)‖z‖² − (2/N)‖z‖⁴ + 2U√N ‖z‖, so a ball is absorbing and a global attractor exists. This is the Stuart–Landau version of the bounded-state lemma coRNN rests on, and it is what the approximation-rate results paper 01 §3.1.4 cites require. It bounds the state, not the gradients.

**P6, the torus lattice.** Periodic boundaries make paper 02's coupling kernel circulant on Z_16 × Z_16, diagonalized by the two-dimensional discrete Fourier transform; the coupling ceiling is the kernel's operator norm, and the stability of the uniform state reads off the kernel's Fourier coefficients ([Sakaguchi, Shinomoto & Kuramoto 1987](https://doi.org/10.1143/PTP.77.1005)). The sheet and the sphere differ from the torus by edge terms carrying a modest fraction of the kernel's weight, which is consistent with the sub-point difference paper 02 measured. A remark, not a theorem, and the reason geometry claims should be made through the kernel spectrum.

## 9. The three tori

Three different objects were called "the torus" in the conversation, and the reply in turn 23 ran them together.

**The coupling-lattice torus.** Paper 02's torus is a boundary condition: a 16 × 16 grid of oscillators whose top row couples to its bottom row and whose left column couples to its right. It makes the kernel circulant (P6). Its measured effect against the sheet, cylinder, helix, cube, sphere, coil and cochlea is under a point, and the sphere in that comparison is an approximation with open poles. Nothing about this torus is a claim about representation; it is a choice of which oscillators are neighbours.

**The N-torus of phases.** N phase oscillators have N angles, so their joint state lives on T^N whatever the coupling is. A closed trajectory that winds p times one way and q times another is a torus knot, which in dynamics is a p:q mode-locked periodic orbit; a trajectory with incommensurate frequencies fills the torus and is quasiperiodic. Neither is "a learned concept"; both are what any two coupled oscillators do.

**The grid-cell activity torus.** The joint firing of the grid cells of one module lies on a two-dimensional torus, in a box, on a track and in sleep ([Gardner et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04268-7)). The standard explanation is a continuous attractor network whose connectivity is built on a twisted torus so that a bump of activity can move across it ([Guanella, Kiper & Verschure 2007](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129065707001093); [Burak & Fiete 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000291)). That is the one place a coupling-lattice torus and an activity torus are linked, and they are linked by a bump attractor, which paper 02's random-kernel lattice does not have. The oscillations paper the reply cited as support ([di Sarra, Jha & Roudi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012776)) shows the activity torus is destroyed by jittering spike times on the theta timescale and is reproduced by independent rate-modulated units with no lattice at all: oscillations are sufficient for the manifold, and lattice geometry is not necessary.

**What geometry can claim.** Through the kernel spectrum, which is what P6 says and what paper 02 measured. A ring or torus attractor claim requires the connectivity that builds a bump, and that is what E1 and E5b test on the fly's and the zebrafish's heading circuits, where the ring exists in the wiring.

## 10. Rhythmic control, and the pathology experiments

**The three controls, and where each is in paper 01.** Dissipation keeps the state bounded (P5) and is what the trainability and approximation results of the coRNN family lean on (paper 01 §3.1.4). Local inhibition, negative coupling from a neighbourhood, is the anti-coupling the conversation asked for; in a connectome it is the sign class of the GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons, and in the fly the APL neuron of the mushroom body and the Δ7 neurons of the central complex are the worked examples. Slow periodic modulation of μ_i or of K, at a frequency well below the units' own, is the model-side version of a theta reset; in the clinical literature it is coordinated reset ([Tass 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00422-003-0425-7)), and in the neuroscience it is the theta phase that gates gamma ([Lisman & Jensen 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.03.007); [Fries 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.034)). The modulation acts on parameters, not on the state, so that P3's structure survives and the time scales stay explicit.

**The pathology map, pre-specified.** The conversation's test was to build the network with these controls, remove them one at a time, and see the diseases. For that to mean anything the signature has to be named before the run, measured by one quantity, and compared with a sham perturbation of equal size applied to a random parameter, so that "we broke it and it broke" cannot count. A result counts only if the predicted measure moves and the sham's does not, in at least four of five seeds.

| Perturbation | Biological analogue | Predicted signature | Measure | Sham |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Remove the slow modulation of μ_i or K | Loss of theta gating; thalamocortical dysrhythmia ([Llinás et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.96.26.15222)) | Activity persists after the input stops; the network stops moving between states | Mean amplitude over the two seconds after input offset; number of distinct cluster states per second | Shift the modulation's phase instead of removing it |
| Raise the coupling within one frequency band | Parkinsonian beta locking | The band's order parameter goes to one; the network stops following its input; a narrowband spectral peak | r(t) in the band; phase-locking value to the input; peak-to-broadband power ratio | Raise the coupling in a random subset of equal size |
| Remove local inhibition (the APL, the Δ7 neurons, or the inhibitory part of a kernel) | Loss of sparseness; seizure-like recruitment | Sparseness lost: the active fraction of the mushroom body rises from about 5% toward 1; the heading bump widens to the whole ring | Active fraction; bump width; r | Remove as many excitatory edges |
| Cut dissipation (lower the damping, raise μ_i) | Runaway excitation | Runaway amplitude or chaos; a broadband rise in power | Largest Lyapunov exponent from trajectory divergence; slope of the power spectrum | The same change in a random parameter |
| Remove the slow modulation in one sensory module only | Tinnitus-like phantom percept | That module keeps a self-sustained pattern with no input | Amplitude and r in the module after input offset | The same removal in a non-sensory module |

**Caveats that bound the claim.** Thalamocortical dysrhythmia, Parkinsonian beta, tinnitus and the demyelination result ([Dubey et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.73827)) are mammalian; the fly has no thalamus and no theta. The table therefore runs on the chained lattice of E0b as generic dynamics, on the fly's mushroom body only for the inhibition row (the fly's odour-evoked oscillations are mediated by GABAergic interneurons and are picrotoxin-sensitive, [Tanaka, Ito & Stopfer 2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1455-09.2009)), and on mammalian wiring only at region level, where Hopf models of Parkinson's stimulation ([Saenger et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-10003-y)) and of epilepsy ([Jirsa et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.04.049)) already exist and the exercise is replication. The dataset survey (Section 11b) found no synaptic-resolution connectome anywhere with co-registered rhythm recordings, so the synaptic-scale pathology test has no biological ground truth yet; its value is internal consistency and the predictions it makes for when such data exist.

## 11. Chaining, and the connectome as a prior

**The architecture.** Modules of dense local coupling (paper 02's channels are the existing example: four 16 × 16 lattices, each with its own kernel), joined by sparse long-range coupling that decays with distance, which is how cortex is wired ([Ercsey-Ravasz et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.07.036)), and organized by cell-type blocks, which is how the fly is wired (8,453 cell types, [Schlegel et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07686-5)). The conversation's "myelin gate" M_ij is the inter-module coupling matrix; its sparsity and its distance dependence are the design variables, and T1.4 is the claim that a chain of such modules beats one dense module at matched states.

**Prior art, with its legitimacy line.** The models that have put real wiring to work, in the order the program meets them. [Lappalainen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07939-3) (Nature, peer-reviewed; Tübingen and the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems): the fly's optic-lobe motion pathway as a deep mechanistic network of threshold-linear units with measured connectivity for 64 cell types, 45,669 neurons and 1,513,231 connections across 721 columns, 734 free parameters fitted by backpropagation to an optic-flow task in a 50-model ensemble, matching 26 experimental studies (code: flyvis). [Shiu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9) (Nature, peer-reviewed; Berkeley): the whole brain as leaky integrate-and-fire units on the FlyWire connectome with neurotransmitter signs, one synaptic weight calibrated against a firing-rate target, predictions for feeding and grooming validated optogenetically. [Jin et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997) (arXiv, preprint; Tsinghua) and its NeurIPS 2025 predecessor, flyGNN (Jin & Sui, a poster in the Data on the Brain and Mind workshop, not a main-track paper): the connectome as a directed message-passing graph controlling a biomechanical fly body, with higher sample efficiency than degree-preserving rewired graphs, random graphs and multilayer perceptrons. [Ódor, Papp & Deco 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chaos.2026.119150) (Chaos, Solitons & Fractals 213:119150, peer-reviewed; Centre for Energy Research, Budapest, and Universitat Pompeu Fabra, Barcelona): Kuramoto on the fly connectome as a criticality study (fluctuation–dissipation, aging, graph dimension about 6, spectral dimension about 2), no task; the group's earlier connectome-Kuramoto papers are peer-reviewed ([Ódor & Kelling 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-54769-9); [Ódor, Deco & Kelling 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.023057); [Ódor et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2023.1150246)). Un-0 and its sparsity post (blog, not peer-reviewed). And the caution from the other direction: [Caranzano et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag425) (Briefings in Bioinformatics, peer-reviewed), who find biologically informed sparsity masks no better than random masks at matched sparsity, which is the reduced claim T2.4 collapses to if the within-type rewired twin ties.

**The twin that decides it.** Rewiring every synapse destroys every pathway, so beating that twin only shows pathways exist. Rewiring within cell-type blocks, preserving in-degree, out-degree and sign class, keeps the pathways and destroys only the fine wiring. A connectome result must beat that twin to be a result about the connectome.

## 12. The experiment program

Ordered as agreed: the existing harness first, then the fly from its smallest circuit to the whole brain, then the other nervous systems. Every experiment states its arms, its pre-specified criterion, what falsifies the claim it serves, and its cost. Arms common to every connectome experiment: the connectome coupling W; the degree-preserving rewired twin within sign class and, where cell types exist, within cell-type blocks; a random twin at the same density and sign fraction; the uncoupled network (the input-only floor); leaky integrate-and-fire and threshold-linear twins on the same W (Shiu's and Lappalainen's units); a size-matched GRU and S4D at the arm's trained-parameter count; a frozen twin wherever anything trains; one spectral-norm cap on W and one readout (paper 02's windowed statistics into a ridge) for all; five seeds.

**E0, harness-local, nearly free.** On paper 02's harness and tasks (spoken digits at clean, 0 and −5 dB, speaker-disjoint; temporal order), with its controls.

- **E0a, operating point (T1.3).** Re-run the coupling-ceiling and restoring-strength sweeps recording the order parameter r (paper 03's measure) and the information processing capacity of [Dambre et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00514) alongside accuracy. Criterion: the optimum sits at r in [0.3, 0.8] with falls on both sides. Falsified if accuracy is flat in r or best at r near 1.
- **E0b, unit zoo (T1.1, T1.2, T1.4 chaining arm).** Every "screen" row of Section 6 at M = 2,048 states: 2,048 Kuramoto phases; 1,024 Stuart–Landau; 683 three-dimensional Stuart–Landau with neutral latitude; 683 Hindmarsh–Rose; 512 four-dimensional Stuart–Landau; 512 four-dimensional Kuramoto; 1,024 second-order units; 2,048 theta neurons. Untrained first, with r, capacity, the largest Lyapunov exponent and FLOPs per step recorded. The chaining arm compares four dense 16 × 16 channels joined by sparse inter-channel coupling against one dense module of 1,024. Criterion: a unit earns a place in E1 only if it beats Stuart–Landau by more than the seed spread on at least one task without instability. Falsified (T1.4) if the dense module or the rewired inter-module twin reads as well as the chain.
- **E0c, training zoo (T1.2, T1.5).** The 1,024-unit Stuart–Landau network trained by each arm of Section 7 (backpropagation through time with Adam; equilibrium propagation, with the local μ_i rule of P3 and with μ_i frozen; contrastive Hebbian learning; Hebbian phase plasticity plus readout; frequency learning; deep-equilibrium at a fixed point in the rotating frame), at matched trained parameters and wall-clock, under the five controls, with the incidence of catastrophic lock and amplitude death. Criterion: a local method within the seed spread of backpropagation at equal parameters. Falsified (T1.5) if backpropagation matches every local method; falsified (T1.2) if a fixed tanh or ReLU gate at matched states ties learned μ_i.
- **E0d, decodability (T1.1).** The toy superposition task of [Elhage et al. 2022](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html) at matched M: are the planted features linearly decodable from (cos θ_i, sin θ_i, |z_i|), from |z_i| alone, and from a scalar layer with and without a sparse autoencoder? Criterion: phase adds decodable features over amplitude alone, and the oscillator layer needs no autoencoder where the scalar layer does. Falsified if reading amplitude alone equals reading amplitude and phase.
- **E0e, resonator control.** A damped linear resonator bank (μ_i < 0, no cubic term) beside every Stuart–Landau result in E0a to E0d. The limit cycle earns credit only for what the resonator cannot do.

Cost: all of E0 fits the M1 Max; paper 03's measurements put a dense 1,024-unit channel at 0.14 ms per clip on the GPU and 4,096 units at 0.90 ms (its cost model, `SIM_MS` in `plan.py`), and paper 02's full 8,048-clip run at 17 seconds.

**E1, the central-complex heading ring (T2.4, and the unit question).** The hemibrain's EPG, PEN_a, PEN_b, Δ7, PEG and EL neurons (46, 20, 22, 42, 18 and 18, from the paper's Table 4) with their ring-neuron inputs, about 170 core cells and about 430 with inputs ([Hulse et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.66039)). Target: the bump of activity persists in darkness and shifts with angular velocity ([Seelig & Jayaraman 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14446); [Kim et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aal4835); [Turner-Evans et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.23496)); existing models: a spiking model of the insect heading circuit ([Pisokas, Heinze & Webb 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.53985)) and an exact ring attractor derived from the fly connectome ([Biswas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.11.01.621596), bioRxiv, still a preprint in its March 2026 revision). Criterion: EPG population-vector length above 0.5 for at least 10 s after the input stops, and tracking error against integrated angular velocity, for the connectome better than both twins. The bump is an amplitude code, so a phase-only ring cannot hold it; Stuart–Landau with per-cell-type μ_i (a budget like Lappalainen's per-type time constants), FitzHugh–Nagumo and the theta neuron run as alternates at matched states. Falsified if the within-type rewired twin ties; the claim then becomes "cell-type block structure is a good prior". Cost: minutes on the M1 Max; the budget is trial length (thousands of steps for persistence) times trials times seeds times arms.

**E2, antennal lobe to mushroom body (T1.1, T1.2, T2.4).** About 150 projection neurons from 51 glomeruli onto about 2,000 Kenyon cells (1,927 in the hemibrain's right hemisphere) with 5.6 dendritic claws each on average, and one APL neuron ([Li et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.62576)). Target: a sparse, decorrelated odour code with about 5% of Kenyon cells active, enforced by APL feedback ([Lin et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3660)). Criterion (T1.1): similar-odour discrimination at matched states, 2,000 Stuart–Landau units (4,000 states) against 4,000 threshold-linear units on the same wiring; the oscillator arm must clear the rate arm by its seed spread, and reading |z| alone must not equal reading (|z|, θ). The APL lesion is the inhibition row of Section 10. Honest caveat: Kenyon-cell-to-Kenyon-cell coupling is thin, so the mushroom body is a feedforward expansion plus one inhibitory neuron, and whatever oscillation adds must come from the APL loop and the projection-neuron drive; the fly's own mushroom-body oscillations ([Tanaka, Ito & Stopfer 2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1455-09.2009)) are the one fly-native hook for Tier 2's rhythm claims. Cost: about 2,200 units dense, seconds per arm.

**E3, the optic-lobe motion pathway (T2.4).** The 45,669-neuron motion pathway against Lappalainen's fitted rate model, with the oscillator unit fitted the same way (backpropagation to the optic-flow task) in an ensemble of five rather than fifty. Criterion: match or beat the rate model on the 26-study activity benchmark at matched parameters. Honest prediction: most optic-lobe neurons are graded and non-oscillatory, so this is the experiment the oscillator unit is most likely to lose; a tie is a null and a loss is public. Run after E1 and E2 fix the unit. Cost: sparse W of 1.5 million edges is cheap per step; the fit is flyvis's own budget.

**E4, the whole fly brain (T2.4, scaling).** 139,255 neurons and 54.5 million synapses ([Dorkenwald et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y)), sparse only; `torch.sparse` has no Metal backend, so this runs on a CUDA pod or on CPU. Task: Shiu's sugar-sensing to MN9 feeding circuit, which must reproduce in sign and in the order of top responders. The hard twin is within-type rewiring. The novelty is the unit, not the prior: FlyGM already shows a trained connectome graph beating rewired and random twins on sample efficiency. Caution from the experiment review: Shiu's model is calibrated by one synaptic weight against a firing-rate target, and an oscillator has no firing rate, so the phase-and-amplitude-to-rate map is a second free choice that can absorb any outcome unless it is fixed before the run. Cost: per step about edges times batch times two fields; hours on a 24 GB pod, days on CPU.

**E5, beyond the fly**, in this order unless reordered. **E5a** the stomatogastric pyloric circuit: about 30 neurons with literature-reconstructed signed wiring; target, triphasic phase constancy across cycle periods (Bucher, Prinz & Marder 2005); arms, Stuart–Landau, three-dimensional Stuart–Landau, theta neuron and a Hodgkin–Huxley reference against rewired wiring; the cheapest decisive test of whether phase-and-amplitude units reproduce a known rhythm from wiring alone. **E5b** the zebrafish hindbrain heading ring and oculomotor integrator: the vertebrate mirror of E1; persistence time and bump stability against calcium data; rewired twins. **E5c** C. elegans: 302 units with signed chemical coupling and symmetric gap-junction coupling; targets, Kato's cyclic manifold and Randi's pairwise propagation measurements; the gap-junction term is the natural home for symmetric coupling and energy-based training. **E5d** the MICrONS proofread subgraph: movie-evoked input; like-to-like response correlations against rewired twins. The pathology table gets a mammalian anchor only at region level, as replication of an established field.

**Likely outcomes, stated now so they can be checked later** (from the experiment review). E1: Stuart–Landau on the connectome holds a bump in a band of μ and coupling ceiling, and so does the within-type rewired twin, because the attractor needs only local excitation and broad inhibition, which block structure supplies; the specific wiring improves tracking at most; phase-only Kuramoto holds no bump. E2: at matched states the oscillator mushroom body ties the rate one, and the phase channel adds one or two points under noise, as in paper 02. E3: the oscillator optic lobe fits the activity benchmark worse than threshold-linear units. E4: the whole brain reproduces Shiu's signs only after the rate map is tuned. The pathology table mostly holds on the lattice because it is generic physics and does not transfer to the fly circuits. The three results that would embarrass the program: within-type twins tie everywhere (T2.4 reduces to cell-type sparsity, which Caranzano, FlyGM and Un-0 already say); |z| alone reads as well as (|z|, θ) (T1.1 is empty and the oscillator is paper 02's leaky integrator with a radius); and equilibrium propagation fails on directed wiring while symmetrizing it to make it work removes the connectome.

## 15. Terminology

Replacements the notes make, so that the theorem is stated in the words the fields use.

- "Localized Lagrangian", "least action" → energy function E (a Lyapunov function), gradient flow, attractor basin; exists only under P3's assumptions (symmetric K, common frequency).
- "Feature sink" → attractor: a fixed point in the rotating frame or a phase-locked cluster; "potential well" only when E exists; a cluster attractor under higher-order coupling.
- "Toroidal knot" → a p:q mode-locked periodic orbit on the invariant torus; "the manifold" → the invariant torus or the quasiperiodic orbit on it.
- "The threshold emerges" → the bifurcation parameter μ_i, and which of fixed, learned, self-tuned or state-dependent is meant.
- "Just before full phase lock" → partial coherence, with the order parameter r measured and reported.
- "Myelin gate M_ij" → sparse, distance-dependent inter-module coupling.
- "Torus" → one of: periodic boundary conditions on the coupling lattice; the N-torus state space of N phases; the population-activity manifold.
- "Theta damping", "theta reset" → slow periodic modulation of μ_i or K; coordinated reset.
- "Beats an equal-sized transformer untrained" → within the trained baselines' range at one gain, below them otherwise (Section 4).
- "Skin effect", "harmonic cancellation" → deleted (Appendix B).
- "Stuart–Landau is only 2D" → Stuart–Landau lacks excitability; the third dimension must say what it is for (Section 6).
- "Category error" for rate or graph models of a connectome → a different unit; those models are dynamical and beat rewired twins.

## 16. Appendix B: quarantined speculation

Tier 3, with the evidence on each side. Nothing in Tiers 1 and 2 depends on any of it. Paper 01 §1.2.2 records the first item as a live minority hypothesis and takes no position, and these notes keep that stance.

**B.1 The action potential as primarily a mechanical and thermodynamic wave.** Claim: the nerve impulse is a density pulse in the lipid membrane near its melting transition, and the electrical signal is secondary ([Heimburg & Jackson 2005](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0503823102)). For it: the heat released during depolarization is reabsorbed during repolarization, so the process is close to adiabatic, which a purely resistive cable is not; the membrane thickens and the axon swells measurably as the impulse passes; general anaesthetics shift the membrane's melting point in a way the model predicts. Against it: tetrodotoxin, which blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, silences propagation, so the channels are causal and not a by-product; the Hodgkin–Huxley conductance model is quantitatively successful over seventy years of experiments; and colliding impulses annihilate in a Hodgkin–Huxley multi-compartment model and in two experimental preparations ([Follmann, Rosa & Stein 2015](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.92.032707)), although penetration was reported in earthworm and lobster giant axons ([Gonzalez-Perez et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.4.031047)) and that report was disputed in a comment ([Berg et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.7.028001)) and defended in a reply ([Wang et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.7.028002)). The synthesis in the literature is coupled electromechanics: an electrical wave and a mechanical wave that drive each other, in which neither is a by-product ([Engelbrecht, Peets & Tamm 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10237-018-1055-2); the fuller treatment is their 2021 monograph, Modelling of Complex Signals in Nerves). The test that would matter: a collision experiment under controlled stimulation that both camps accept, or a mechanical pulse shown to cross a segment in which the sodium channels are blocked.

**B.2 Myelin as a frequency filter; theta penetrating it; theta cancelling gamma.** Claim: myelin shields axons from interfering frequencies, low-frequency theta penetrates it by the skin effect or cancels gamma harmonically. As physics this does not hold. The skin depth of a conductor is (2 / ωμσ)^(1/2); for tissue with conductivity about 0.3 S/m it is about 290 m at 10 Hz and about 90 m at 100 Hz, so at brain scales every field in these bands penetrates everything, and extracellular potentials below 100 Hz are quasi-static volume conduction. Myelin changes the cable properties of the axon it wraps (lower capacitance, higher membrane resistance), which is why conduction is fast and saltatory; it does not filter fields. Theta (4 to 8 Hz) and gamma (30 to 90 Hz) are not harmonically related in general; what exists is phase–amplitude coupling, in which gamma amplitude varies with theta phase, with no cancellation. The demyelination result that was attached to this claim is real: in the cuprizone model, gamma is abolished and theta amplified, with interictal discharges; its mechanism is the loss of parvalbumin-interneuron axon myelination and hence of fast phasic inhibition, shown by optogenetic rescue ([Dubey et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.73827)). What survives is "myelination of inhibitory interneurons supports gamma", which is Dubey's finding and belongs in Tier 2.

**B.3 A mechanical synapse.** Claim: the impulse's mechanical component helps release vesicles, and its trailing rarefaction drives reuptake. No paper stating the hypothesis in the reply's form was found. The nearest published work is a demonstration that a mechanical push on a presynaptic bouton, from the enlargement of a dendritic spine, raises evoked release ([Ucar et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04125-7)), since called mechanical transmission, and, from the soliton side, an argument that nerve contraction would make fast mechanical synapses possible ([Heimburg 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemphyslip.2024.105461)), with no vesicle-release claim. Either is compatible with ordinary channel-driven transmission and neither requires B.1. Ions do not cross lipid without channels, so the mechanical component, if it acts, acts on channels and on vesicle positioning. Test: mechanosensitive-channel blockade with the electrical signal intact.

**B.4 Quantum mechanics in neural computation.** Claim: neurons compute quantum mechanically, or "it's fluid and quantum mechanics". Two readings. In the weak reading, ion channels, membranes and transmitters are quantum-chemical objects, which is true of all chemistry and carries no computational consequence. In the strong reading, coherent quantum states take part in computation; the decoherence times for superpositions in the warm, wet brain are of the order of 10^(−13) to 10^(−20) s against neural time scales of 10^(−3) s ([Tegmark 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194); [Koch & Hepp 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/440611a)), the microtubule proposal ([Hameroff & Penrose 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2013.08.002)) has not survived that objection in the view of most of the field, and the most careful version, nuclear spins in phosphate molecules as long-lived qubits ([Fisher 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aop.2015.08.020)), is a proposal with specific experiments attached and, as far as these notes know, no positive result yet.
